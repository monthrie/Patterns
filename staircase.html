A square needs 25 cells to carry five strings. The staircase does it with 13 — each three-cell step of the stair cages exactly one loop, a little four-bounce diamond. Add a step, gain a loop, forever.

The same law caps every small board: no shape with up to 14 cells beats ⌊(n+2)/3⌋ loops, and at 7, 10 and 13 cells the record-holder is this staircase and nothing else.

Five loops = five colours. This board has never been woven by anyone. But notice: its loops never cross. Maximum fragmentation means zero weave — five separate diamonds resting on a board. As cloth, it falls apart. The mathematical extreme is the craft minimum.
