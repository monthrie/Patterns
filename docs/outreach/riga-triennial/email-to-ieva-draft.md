# Draft email to Ieva Zvejniece (Riga Triennial) — passionate version

**To:** ieva.zvejniece@lnmm.gov.lv  (cc vita.birzaka@lnmm.gov.lv optional)
**Subject:** Seeking your advice on an extraordinary craft

---

Dear Ieva,

I am writing because I have witnessed one of the most extraordinary crafts I have ever seen, and I am trying to find the right place in the world to show it.

His name is João das Cordas, a Portuguese master knotmaker and a member of the International Guild of Knot Tyers. Entirely self-taught, over decades of trial and error, he has invented a completely novel way of making textiles. What he makes is not woven, not knitted, not crocheted. It is built from closed knots, Celtic knots, each one a single unbroken loop, interlaced with the others and then spliced back to itself so finely that you cannot find the join.

You can read the whole idea in his shoes. A pair of his black and white slippers is made of exactly two closed loops. A rainbow pair is made of twelve, one loop for every colour. And his wool jacket, which took him more than two hundred hours, is four such knots perfectly interwoven into a garment with no seam, no stitch, and no thread that has an end. I have honestly never seen anything like it.

The underlying geometry may be more than a thousand years old, echoed in the sand drawings of Angola, but as far as anyone has been able to discover, no one before João has found a way to make three dimensional objects, shoes, waistcoats, a jacket, from these knots. He even designed his own marked paper and built all of his own tooling to make it possible. Each shoe begins as a flat shaped panel woven on a board of nails, which he then forms into three dimensions, and I have attached photographs of that process too.

Here is my one difficulty, and the real reason I am writing rather than simply applying. The most astonishing thing about each piece is how it is made, and that is almost invisible in a single photograph. I believe it can be conveyed, through a detail image, a few careful words, and perhaps a short film of the process, but I would love the guidance of someone who truly knows this field.

So I wanted to ask you directly: is João's work something that could belong in the Riga Triennial? And if you feel it is not the right home, is there somewhere you would suggest? I would also value your instinct on whether to show the jacket, a wearable three dimensional piece, or a large rug in a single, very clear pattern.

One note: João does not use the internet, but he is delighted to write about his process and to answer anything at all.

I have attached a few images to give a first sense of it. Thank you for reading this far.

With warm regards,
[your name]
