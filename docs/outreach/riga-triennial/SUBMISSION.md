## Concept (500–800 chars, publication-ready) — current draft
> This jacket has no seam, no stitch, and no thread with an end. It was woven in eight flat panels of wool, tied into a single three-dimensional garment, and then traced through, loop by loop, while it already held the shape of a body, each cord following its whole path and joined back to its own beginning so finely the join cannot be found. The maker designs only the boundary; a cord turning at every edge does the rest. Knowing nothing of the mathematics, João das Cordas had woven what knot theory calls a link: closed curves holding one another in place. Two hundred hours, and not one of them at a loom. It is a drawing that became a body, at the threshold between two dimensions and three.
