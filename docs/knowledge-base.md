## 2. Craft mechanics & hard-won practical truths

- Board: nails along the boundary of a cell-grid shape; cord travels at 45°, bounces at edges, returns to start; multiple loops cover the board; crossings alternate over/under (checkerboard parity).
- **VERIFIED (engine test, 2026-06-11): the checkerboard parity ≡ strict alternation along each cord** — over, under, over, under at successive crossings, *through every bounce*, closing consistently when the loop returns home. Held with zero exceptions on rectangles, L-shapes, holes (664 strand-crossings on 18×10 alone). **This is why the craft is humanly possible**: over/under needs no global picture, only rhythm (darning cadence), and the closure can never clash. Bonus: alternating = the strongest weave (plain-weave binding) AND the knot-theoretically minimal diagram (Tait) — easiest, strongest, most canonical are the same rule.
- **The code inherited the paper (Wilmon, 2026-06-11)**: the build order inside the original prototype was (1) get a loop to come back to its start; (2) **place the basket weave UNDER the knot for the code to glance at for over/under** — the same method as João's paper, adopted directly. The function wasn't translated into code; it was inherited as a layer. (Photo of the paper on a nail board received 2026-06-11 — in the new photo drops.)
- **The paper GENERATOR is in the codebase**: `archive/niche-tools/weave-pattern.html` ("Diagonal Basket Weave" — size controls, shape editor, printable canvas). Wilmon built it so João could produce the paper he needs; **before it, João drew the sheet by hand and photocopied it**. Three tool generations: hand-drawn + photocopier → generator → app. ⚠ The generator is a *working* tool (paper is a consumable, one sheet per new board) — confirm João still uses it; if so it deserves promotion out of `archive/` and a place in the site's tool story.
- **How João ACTUALLY decides over/under (Wilmon, 2026-06-11): he designed and produced his own BASKET-WEAVE PAPER** — taped to the board, nails driven through it. **Reading the paper (Wilmon's precise account): the little CIRCLES mark where nails go if the boundary passes there (the bounce/gap lattice); the DASHES show under-or-over at each crossing (the parity).** One sheet therefore encodes the craft's entire apparatus — boundary slots + crossing answers for ANY shape drawn on it — the exact analog of the engine's `buildGaps` + `(k+m+W)%2`. A positional lookup, so it answers even at crossings "floating in nowhere" (the answer is a property of position, not history). Photo received 2026-06-11 (L-shaped boundary, hand-lettered edge labels in blue/red).
- **Why a lookup was NECESSARY (engine simulation, 2026-06-11)**: simulating chronological laying, the live over/under decisions (only at crossings where a strand is already present) form an *irregular* sequence (e.g. `UOOUOUUUO…`) — no human rhythm could track it. Pure alternation is a property of the *finished* weave (verified, see above), not a usable laying procedure. João's paper is the only memoryless method, and he invented it.

## 3. The mathematics — established results

(Sources with verified links: `docs/references.md` — papers, books, primary ethnography, image sources with licenses.)

- The paths are **mirror curves** (Gerdes/Jablan terminology), the geometry of the **sona** sand drawings of the Chokwe people, NE Angola; same family generates Celtic knotwork and Tamil kolam.
- **gcd theorem**: an m×n rectangle needs exactly gcd(m,n) loops (Gerdes; also classical via arithmetic billiards).
  - Corollaries: all loops of a rectangle have **equal length** ∝ lcm(m,n). A **square n×n needs exactly n loops** (maximal case — the heatmap diagonal).
  - **Distribution**: probability two random sides are coprime = 6/π² ≈ 61% ⇒ **monolinear is the COMMON case for rectangles**, not rare. (Correction logged — see §5.)
- **Merge/split rule**: an obstacle/mirror at a crossing of two *different* loops always fuses them (count −1); at a self-crossing it splits or preserves (Chavey–Straffin).
- **Universality**: mirror curves with alternating crossings realize *every* knot and link (Jablan, Radović, Sazdanović, Zeković — equivalence with knot mosaics/grid diagrams).
- **Open problem**: no closed-form loop count for general polyominoes (L-shapes, holes, notches). Only clean general result: scaling law **L(kP) = k·L(P)**. Posed as unsolved as recently as 2020 (Matthew Yuan, "Polyomino Loops" — ⚠ a Medium essay, NOT a refereed paper; never say "in print". See `docs/references.md`).
- 3D cousins: **billiard knots** (Jones & Przytycki; Lissajous knots; Lamm; Koseleff & Pecker: every knot is a billiard knot in some prism).
- Each finished piece is, strictly, an **alternating link** with N components (jacket = probably 4 components, uncounted — see §1 correction; rainbow shoes = 12-component, b/w shoes = 2-component).

## 4. Lineage & novelty assessment (researched, cited in essay)

- Historical chain: **diagonally plaited mats → sona sand drawings → mathematics**. Gerdes established the drawings descend from mat-strand paths.
- **Sona was inscribed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (Angola, 2023)** — "Sona, drawings and geometric figures on sand" ([listing](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/sona-drawings-and-geometric-figures-on-sand-01994)). Not yet in the essay; strengthens the lineage section and the site's credibility. Listing photos © rights reserved — link, don't reuse.
- Crucial distinctions vs the mats (so "someone did it before" is wrong):
  - Mat-makers worked **locally** (crossing by crossing); João works **globally** (one whole closed cycle at a time).
  - Mat strands are finite, spliced, with ends — the **closed loop was the sand drawings' idealization**, never physically realized in textile. João's invisible self-joins make it physically true, apparently for the first time.
  - Mats were rectangles only; João works in the open-problem territory (irregular boundaries, holes).
- Chokwe aesthetics prized **monolinearity** (one line = mastery; many lines = lesser/corrupted variant). João held the same ideal independently, then inverted it via accidents → loops-as-colours.
- **No software precedent**: closest is Chavey's educational sona Java tool (Beloit). Nothing combines arbitrary boundaries + billiard tracing + over/under weave + physical string lengths + fabrication output.
- **No craft precedent found**: no documented tradition weaving mirror-curve paths as textile on nail boards. Honest claim wording: "as far as we have been able to discover, no documented precedent."
- Most of his multi-cycle patterns on irregular boards are plausibly **first sightings by any human** (only computable by simulation; astronomically large shape space).
- Key people: **Paulus Gerdes** (1952–2014), **Slavik Jablan** (1952–2015), **Darrah Chavey** (d. 6 Jan 2024, verified 2026-06-11 via Beloit obituary — was on our living list once; papers remain citable) — all deceased. Living/active in the lineage: Ljiljana Radović (Niš), Radmila Sazdanović (NC State), Józef Przytycki (GWU), Christoph Lamm, P.-V. Koseleff, D. Pecker; plus the mathematical-fiber-arts community (belcastro/Yackel, new journal *Interlace*) and Gerdes' student Marcos Cherinda (UNESCO Maputo).
